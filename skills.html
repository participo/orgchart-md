New skills. A new challenge

Three skills have emerged to make this work: delegation, discernment, debugging. Delegation is knowing what to hand off, and how. Discernment is taste applied to what the agent produced. Debugging is figuring out why it went wrong.

The problem: each skill atrophies the capacity it depends on. Delegate enough production and you lose the contextual knowledge that made you good at delegating. Skip enough evaluation practice and your discernment hollows out. Outsource your domain understanding and you can detect something's wrong but no longer say why. Intellectual debt. The mid-career professional is most exposed: too far past onboarding to benefit from the lift, not yet senior enough to amplify accumulated expertise.

Is the solution deliberate friction? Rotate people through AI-heavy and AI-light work. Preserve mentorship on tasks agents could handle. Build "show your work" checkpoints that slow throughput but preserve learning. Some friction is load-bearing.

Are these "3D skills" durable? Will "friction" counteract atrophy? Here's what the research agents have learnt.
