A kanban for the agent, checked out by people

This site is a working record of the framework being tested. The framework makes specific claims; the daily pipeline tracks the evidence as it shows up in the wild, both the parts that support the framework and the parts that push back. Each item lives on the relevant layer page with its tags visible. Trajectory tags update as the picture changes.

The pipeline itself runs on the practices the framework describes. The five columns below are the kanban; reading them left-to-right is the day-in-the-life. The agent owns triage and staging; people own judgement and the commit.

Why this approach?

A kanban for tracking work in progress. Structured handoffs between agent and people. An explicit power-tag rubric standing in for standard work. Root-cause logic when triage drops items. Periodic review cycles for trajectory updates.

The medium is markdown; the substrate is the application of timeless lean/agile disciplines. The framework's key ideas used to drive this site's own production.

Supervising from above

The agent owns the daily triage and staging. People supervise by reviewing, promoting, and setting trajectory. Accountability for what ends up on the site is with people; the agent holds accountability for finding out whether it's working.

The argument under test is whether operationally-disciplined deployment of agents produces durable advantage over a tooling-and-talent approach. The pipeline tracks the evidence as it shows up, supports and challenges in the same view.
