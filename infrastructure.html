layer 03 · management as infrastructure

The forgotten operating system

What's unlocking the latest agent capabilities? Kanban boards. Explicit handoff protocols. Delegation with scope and boundaries. Root-cause analysis. Memory and learning loops. Specs and skill files as durable operating protocols.

None of this is new. Frontier firms are rediscovering and applying late-20th-century management practices that many organisations let atrophy.

Of course, lean and agile weren't invented to manage agents. They were invented to manage work across people and machines under uncertainty, where production was faster than coordination could keep up; the very conditions agent-infused work now operates in. Visible work-in-progress on a board. Root-cause analysis when something fails. Memory that survives the task that generated it. With an operational substrate, agent throughput compounds into capability that can connect across the horizontal work of an organisation.

Will "dusting off" lean and agile practices fix the absorption gap caused by the exponential capability of agents? Here's where the evidence is stacking up.

  • Brandon Gell & Willie Williams, Every·evidence

    Every rolled out a personal agent per employee, abandoned it within weeks, rebuilt around shared coworkers with explicit roles, group access, and group memory.

    Plus One launched after a Panama retreat as a personal-agent-per-employee deployment on Every's OpenClaw harness. The architecture was abandoned within weeks because the agents were unreliable, broke constantly, and required more upkeep than the productivity they returned. Plus One 2.0 is being rebuilt around shared, reliable coworkers with explicit roles, scoped group access, and memory accumulated from group interactions. Real-time empirical case of the management-as-infrastructure prescription being learned through failure: the team discovered they needed explicit handoff protocols, scoped delegation, and durable shared memory, exactly the operational substrate the layer prescribes.

  • John Cutler, The Beautiful Mess·evidence

    TBM 422: Exception, Presence, Delegation as the three-motion working repertoire of management as infrastructure.

    Cutler proposes a three-motion triad as the back-to-basics management vocabulary the agentic moment calls for. Exception-based systems flag deviation from baseline and learn from the exceptions themselves; presence-based work transfers tacit knowledge that documentation cannot capture; delegation pushes authority to information rather than information to authority. The three create a virtuous loop and each fails when run without the other two; Mulally at Ford, Chesky at Airbnb, and Huang at Nvidia are the worked examples.

−challenges the claim 3 items
  • SkillSafetyBench authors, arXiv cs.AI / cs.MA·evidence

    Even with benign user requests, task-relevant skill materials can steer agents toward unsafe actions.

    155 adversarial cases across 47 tasks, 6 risk domains, 30 safety categories, multiple CLI agents and model backends. "Localized non-user attacks can consistently induce unsafe behavior, with distinct failure patterns across domains, attack methods, and scaffold-model pairings." Direct counter-evidence to the framework's assumption that markdown skill files are neutral durable carriers. The properties that make skill files durable (text, addressable, transferable, consumed by downstream computation) make them a transferable attack surface. The Layer 3 prescription needs a trust discipline for skill files that has no clean late-20th-century antecedent; the closest analogue is dependency hygiene in software supply chains, itself a 21st-century discipline still being figured out.
