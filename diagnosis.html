layer 01 · the diagnosis

Agents are vertical. Organisations are horizontal

Human work flows sideways: across people, review loops, teams, and steercos. Each handoff in those horizontal workflows was designed for human speed and manual oversight. What used to require multiple steps, and handoffs to other apps, colleagues or teams, agents have collapsed into integrated, vertical tasks.

The mismatch creates an AI absorption and coordination "tax" now that agents transform AI into a capability that's producing more coherently, and faster than organisations can process, and integrate. People in existing horizontal workflows carry a new burden: reviewing, validating, and integrating agent output.

Can organisations adapt? Coordination, context, judgment, and organisational memory all need to land where horizontal work meets vertical agent capability.

A quick, practical diagnostic: People Per Process (PPP). Count the number of people a piece of work touches before completion. Low PPP signals vertical-ready work. High PPP reveals the horizontal organisation. Decompose who's there and why. Some people are load-bearing (core function, real expertise, real approval rights). Other people aren't (awareness, CYA, legacy inclusion). Increasingly, agents' vertical task integration will expose these PPP tensions.

Is the absorption gap compounding? Here's what the research agents are finding.

    More than 80% of code merged into Anthropic's production codebase is now written by Claude; engineers ship roughly 8× more code per quarter than in 2021–2025.

    Anthropic reports that as of May 2026 more than 80% of code merged into its production codebase is written by Claude, with engineers shipping roughly eight times more code per quarter than across 2021 to 2025.   • Demirer, Musolff & Yang (SSRN)·evidence

    Autonomous coding agents lift commits 180%, but the effect falls to 50% for projects and 30% for releases: a weak-link pattern across 100,000+ developers.

    A matched event study over more than 100,000 GitHub developers finds autonomous coding agents raise commits by a cumulative 180%, with the gain attenuating to 50% at the project level and 30% at actual releases. The authors name this a weak-link pattern: agent productivity is throttled by human bottlenecks downstream in the production chain. The result is large-N evidence for the diagnosis that vertical agent output is taxed where it meets the horizontal work of review, integration, and release.

  • Azeem Azhar, Exponential View·evidence

    Why AI isn't showing up on your bottom line.

    Three-stage maturity ladder organised by logic rather than capability: Stage 1 speeds individuals, Stage 2 speeds workflows (the current "agent bolted to existing process" pattern), Stage 3 speeds the firm through closed-loop autonomy, with a "business operating graph" (machine-readable representation of what middle management currently holds in heads, decks, and SaaS) as the unlock. Empirical anchors: only 27% of executives report bottom-line AI impact three-plus years after ChatGPT; the senior exec quote that 1,000 Claude Code engineers produce "1+1+1+1=1.5"; Paul David's electrification analogy (group-drive to unit-drive factory rewiring) as the historical parallel for current bolt-on stagnation. Names OpenAI launching an FDE role, Boyd's OODA, Shein clickstream-to-manufacturing, and Block reorganisation as the operational vocabulary.

  • Anthropic·illustration

    Project Glasswing: an initial update.

    One-month progress on the Glasswing partner programme: Cloudflare reports 2,000 bugs found (400 high or critical) with a false-positive rate better than human testers; Mozilla found and fixed 271 vulnerabilities in Firefox 150 against ~27 in Firefox 148 with Opus 4.6, a tenfold rate increase; UK AISI confirms Mythos Preview is the first model to solve both their end-to-end cyber ranges. The vertical-find collapses into agent capability while the horizontal verify-disclose-patch workflow remains a multi-role human chain that cannot absorb the throughput.
