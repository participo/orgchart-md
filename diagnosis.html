layer 01 · the diagnosis

Agents are vertical. Organisations are horizontal

Agents work end-to-end. They take an idea, build the prototype, draft the analysis, ship the code. Work that used to flow across people, with handoffs, reviews, sign-offs and steercos, can now collapse into a single pass.

Most organisations were not built for that. The coordination machinery inside them was calibrated for human attention as the limit on throughput. When you put vertical agent capability against horizontal organisational structure, you get a faster firehose pointed at the same narrow pipe. The most AI-productive workers report more coordination work, and the impact still doesn't land at the P&L.

A practical diagnostic: People Per Process. Count the number of people a piece of work touches before completion. Low PPP signals vertical-ready work. High PPP shows where the structure absorbs the gain. Decompose who's there and why. Some people are load-bearing (core function, real expertise, real approval rights). Some are sediment (awareness, CYA, legacy inclusion). Increasing agent capability will expose the difference.

Is the absorption gap compounding? Agents keep searching for evidence, either supporting it or pushing back.

Direct empirical evidence that tooling-and-talent does not produce AI-native economics without structural redesign. Numbers warrant independent verification before any further citation.
